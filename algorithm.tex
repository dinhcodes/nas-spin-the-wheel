\begin{abstract}
The wheel shows eight equal-looking slices, but the winner is chosen by a
self-correcting pacing model rather than by the visual. Each prize's draw
probability equals the rate at which it must be handed out so its stock reaches
zero exactly when the event ends; a wildcard ``\texttt{?}'' absorbs whatever
probability the real prizes do not need. This note states the model and lists
the values in use.
\end{abstract}

\section{The model}

\subsection{Effective spin rate}

Let $r_{30}(t)$ be the number of spins recorded in the rolling window
$(t-30\text{min},\,t]$. With auto-pacing on, in event hours, once at least
$m=5$ spins have been observed, the model tracks the live rate; otherwise it
uses the seed $s_0$:
\begin{equation}
s(t) =
\begin{cases}
r_{30}(t), & \text{auto on} \;\wedge\; \text{in event} \;\wedge\; r_{30}(t) \ge m, \\[4pt]
s_0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Expected spins remaining}

The event is partitioned into 30-minute blocks ($\Delta = 30$~min) with start
times $a_k$. For the block in progress the remaining fraction is prorated:
\begin{equation}
f_k(t) = \clip\!\left(\frac{a_k + \Delta - t}{\Delta},\; 0,\; 1\right),
\qquad
R(t) = s(t)\sum_{k} f_k(t).
\end{equation}
Every block carries equal weight, so $\sum_k f_k(t)$ is simply the number of
30-minute blocks left (with the current one prorated).

\subsection{Pace, wildcard, and weights}

Each in-stock prize has a \emph{pace} --- the probability it should be drawn now
so its stock runs out exactly at the end:
\begin{equation}
\rho_i = \frac{q_i}{R(t)}, \qquad i \in \R.
\end{equation}
The wildcard takes whatever probability the real prizes do not require:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
P_? = \clip\!\Big(1 - \textstyle\sum_{i \in \R}\rho_i,\; 0,\; 1\Big)
     = \max\!\Big(0,\; 1 - \tfrac{Q}{R(t)}\Big).
\;}
\end{equation}
The remaining budget $1-P_?$ is split among the real prizes in proportion to a
weight that folds in priority and manual boost:
\begin{equation}
w_i = q_i \,\pi_i \left(1 + \frac{b_i}{100}\right),
\qquad
\boxed{\;
P_i = (1 - P_?)\,\frac{w_i}{\sum_{j \in \R} w_j}.
\;}
\end{equation}
The winner is then sampled from the categorical distribution
$\big(P_i\big)_{i \in \R} \cup \{P_?\}$; the winning prize's stock is decremented
by one (or, for the wildcard, a counter is incremented).

\section{The pace identity}

Note that $P_?$ is computed from raw stock, \emph{not} from the weights --- so
priority and boost never change how much probability goes to ``\texttt{?}'';
they only redistribute it among the real prizes. With defaults
($\pi_i = 1,\; b_i = 0$, hence $w_i = q_i$) and while there is slack
($Q \le R$, so $1-P_? = Q/R$):
\begin{equation}
P_i = \frac{Q}{R}\cdot\frac{q_i}{Q} = \frac{q_i}{R(t)} = \rho_i .
\end{equation}
Every prize is drawn at exactly its own pace, so all stocks reach zero together
at the end. Raising $\pi_i$ above $1$ front-loads prize $i$ (it clears earlier);
once it hits zero its weight vanishes and that budget flows to the rest.

\section{Edge cases}

\begin{align}
\R = \varnothing &\;\Rightarrow\; P_? = 1 \quad\text{(all real prizes gone)},\\
R(t) \le 0 &\;\Rightarrow\; P_? = 0,\quad P_i = \frac{w_i}{\sum_j w_j}
\quad\text{(event over: hand out the rest now)},\\
Q > R(t) &\;\Rightarrow\; P_? = 0 \quad\text{(behind schedule: every spin is a real prize).}
\end{align}

\section{Worked example: the priority front-load}

At full stock the three trials hold $28$ of $128$ units ($21.9\%$), but their
combined weight is $39.2$ of $139.2$:
\begin{equation}
\frac{w_{\text{Hammock}} + w_{\text{Hoop}} + w_{\text{Pole}}}{\sum_j w_j}
= \frac{14 + 14 + 11.2}{139.2} = \frac{39.2}{139.2} \approx 28.2\%
\end{equation}
of every real-prize draw --- roughly $1.29\times$ their natural pace. So the
trials trickle out a little ahead of the commons, while the wildcard rate
$P_?$ (fixed by $1 - Q/R$) is left untouched.
